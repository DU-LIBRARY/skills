CONTROL OURSELVES

The real test of our education is our character. The Prophet ﷺ was humanity's greatest teacher and its finest example of good character: وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍ "And indeed, you are upon an outstanding standard of character." (Sūrat al-Qalam: 4) Good character is how we behave when nobody is watching, how we treat people weaker than us and how we respond when our patience is tested. Islamic teaching even shaped everyday care and hygiene: ḥammāms (public baths), soap made from oils and al-qalī, al-Kindī's 107 methods for making perfume, Ziryāb's early toothpaste in al-Andalus, and al-Zahrāwī's writing on oral care, skin and grooming. As students, we try to: - Respect everyone. Parents, teachers, elders and classmates alike. - Live with integrity. Be truthful, trustworthy and sincere in what we say and promise. - Choose humility. Reject pride, arrogance and looking down on others. - Control ourselves. Manage our anger, be patient, own our mistakes and treat every person with dignity. Good grades may win applause, but good character earns lasting respect. A student with top marks who lacks honesty, humility and manners has missed the point of education.
