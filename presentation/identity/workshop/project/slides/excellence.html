None of it came from doing the minimum.

Islam leaves no room for laziness, carelessness or half-hearted effort. It asks us for iḥsān: excellence, sincerity and skill in everything we do. إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَتَبَ الْإِحْسَانَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ "Indeed, Allah has prescribed excellence in all things." (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim) Look at what Muslim craftsmen and thinkers produced: the geometry of the Alhambra in Spain, Mimar Sinan's domes at the Selimiye Mosque in Edirne and the Süleymaniye Mosque in Istanbul, fine ceramics, lustreware and carpets, the philosophy of Ibn Rushd, the history and sociology of Ibn Khaldūn, and the poetry of Rūmī and Saʿdī. None of it came from doing the minimum. It came from discipline, patience and the wish to do things properly. Whether we are memorising the Qurʾān, solving equations, learning Arabic, playing sport or helping at home, we bring the same spirit of iḥsān. As students, we try to: - Give our best. Approach our studies with focus, punctuality and self-discipline. - Refuse to coast. Never settle for "good enough" when we can do better. - Learn from setbacks. Treat mistakes as steps forward, not reasons to give up. - Stay ambitious and grateful. Aim high while staying humble and thankful to Allah.
