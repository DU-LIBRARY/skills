ON THEIR SHOULDERS

Ibn Sīnā · al-Zahrāwī · al-Khwārizmī · al-Kindī · Ibn al-Haytham · Fāṭimah al-Fihrī

Islam encourages curiosity, reflection and the courage to think. The first word revealed to the Prophet ﷺ was a command to learn: اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ "Read in the name of your Lord Who created." (Sūrat al-ʿAlaq: 1) Muslims who took this command seriously asked bold questions, tested ideas and changed the world. In 9th-century Baghdad, Caliph al-Maʾmūn brought scholars of every faith to the House of Wisdom to translate Greek, Persian and Syriac works. In Fez, Fāṭimah al-Fihrī founded al-Qarawiyyīn, the world's oldest university still operating. Ibn Sīnā wrote medical encyclopaedias that were studied for centuries. Al-Zahrāwī designed more than 200 surgical instruments, many still recognisable in operating theatres today. Jābir ibn Ḥayyān laid the foundations of experimental chemistry. In astronomy, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Ṣūfī recorded the Andromeda galaxy and mapped the stars, and Maryam al-Aṣṭurlābiyya and Ulugh Beg made astrolabes and star tables of great precision. In mathematics and light, al-Khwārizmī gave the world algebra, al-Kindī pioneered codebreaking, al-Bīrūnī worked on mathematical problems, and Ibn al-Haytham transformed optics with his camera obscura experiments. In geography and engineering, al-Idrīsī mapped the world, Ibn Baṭṭūṭa travelled more than 75,000 miles across 44 of today's countries, ʿAbbās ibn Firnās attempted winged flight, and al-Jazarī and Taqī al-Dīn built mechanical clocks, crankshafts and water pumps centuries ahead of their time. They did all this with parchment, ink and hard work. We have every book, laboratory and digital tool at our fingertips. So why shouldn't the next great doctor, engineer, mathematician, researcher or scholar be sitting in this room? As students, we try to: - Love all useful learning. Bring the same energy to the Islamic sciences and to our school subjects. - Question and reflect. Think critically, ask deep questions and aim to understand, not just memorise. - Honour our heritage. Respect our teachers, and treat knowledge as an amānah, a trust to use responsibly. Knowledge without character leads to arrogance. Knowledge guided by faith and humility benefits everyone.
