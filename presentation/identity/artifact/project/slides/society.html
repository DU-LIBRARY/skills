ON THEIR SHOULDERS

The Madīnah covenant · trade routes · paper and libraries · coffee, chess and the miswāk

As Muslims in Britain, we are an active part of the society around us. Being a committed Muslim and contributing to British life support each other. When the Prophet ﷺ established the community of Madīnah, Muslims lived alongside neighbours of other faiths under clear agreements on rights, justice and shared responsibility. Muslim merchants, navigators and thinkers connected Africa, Asia and Europe. They exchanged ideas, improved navigation, spread crops such as citrus fruits, apricots and rice, shared water systems like qanats and irrigation rules that left their mark on Spain, and carried Yemeni coffee around the world, all the way to London's coffeehouses, the "penny universities". As young British Muslims, we try to: - Walk with quiet confidence. Be proud of being Muslim, without cutting ourselves off and without compromising. - Build bridges. Respect people of all faiths and backgrounds and make real friendships, while staying true to our principles. - Stand up for justice. Take responsibility for our local community and speak up for fairness, even when it is unpopular. - Lead by example. Let our courtesy, honesty and warmth show people what Islam looks like. We don't watch from the sidelines. Rooted in our faith, we step forward as trustworthy neighbours, thoughtful citizens and people who work for the common good.
