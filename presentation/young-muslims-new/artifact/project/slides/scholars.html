The Dedication of Our Early Scholars Consider the example of Sayyidunā ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhumā). As a young boy, he was blessed with the Prophet’s ﷺ duʿāʾ for knowledge and understanding. After the Prophet’s passing, his thirst for knowledge led him to the homes of senior Companions, where he would sometimes wait outside in the scorching heat rather than disturb their rest. Through humility, perseverance and dedication, that young boy went on to become Ḥibr al-Ummah, the great scholar of the Ummah. Centuries later, Imām al-Bukhārī (raḥimahullāh) demonstrated that same remarkable dedication. From a young age, he devoted himself to the study of ḥadīth, travelling vast distances to learn from scholars and carefully examining the reliability of narrators. It is related that he would wake repeatedly during the night to record important thoughts and observations. His extraordinary commitment to accuracy and scholarship culminated in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, one of the most celebrated works in Islamic history.
