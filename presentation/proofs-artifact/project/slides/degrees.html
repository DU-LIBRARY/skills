RELATIONS WITH NON-MUSLIMS

Four degrees of closeness

مُوَالَاة

مُوَاسَاة

مُدَارَاة

مُعَامَلَات

muwālāt

Love and close friendship from the heart. Kept for Muslims.

muwāsāt

Sympathy, charity and consolation. Permitted, unless they are at war with Muslims.

mudārāt

Courtesy and friendliness. Permitted with all.

muʿāmalāt

Trade, partnership and employment. Permitted, if it does not harm Muslims.

Qur'anic proofs (continued): I feel that it is fitting here to now discuss the other Quran ayaat which mention muslim non-muslim relations. There are various verses discussing this topic, at a surface level, they may seem to contradict each other and the practice of the Prophet and the rightly guided khulafa. However the truth is that there are different degrees in regards to relations with non-muslims. The first degree is muwālāt. This is having close friendships which entails affection and love which comes from the heart. This sort of relation with non-muslims is impermissible and is meant to be reserved only for fellow muslims. This is the type of relation prohibited in verses like (58:22) The second degree is muwāsāt, a relationship based on sympathy, kindness and concern which entails charitable help, consolation and well-wishing. This type of relation with non-muslims is permissible provided they are not actively at war with the muslims. This has been expressed explicitly in the verses of Surah Mumtahinah (60:8-9) The third degree is mudārāt, a relationship based on showing courtesy, politeness and friendliness. This is permissible with all non-muslims and even encouraged when the intention is to present the faith to them or when they are guests or to protect oneself from harm as Allah says (3:28). The fourth and final degree is mu'āmalāt meaning dealings, whether it be transactions, business partnerships or employment. This is permissible with non-muslims on the condition that it does not harm the general body of muslims, for example selling weapons to non-muslims who are actively in war against the muslims. The permissibility of mu'āmalāt can be seen throughout the sunnah, to the extent that when the Prophet passed away, his armour was in the possession of Jewish man as collateral.
