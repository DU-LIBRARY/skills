First, the uniting factor. In India, ending colonial rule was the one thing that united everyone. We don't live under colonial occupation, so what unites us here is the everyday pressure ordinary people in Britain are under. What are those pressures? Recent UK polling puts the cost of living at the top, at 90%, rising to 94% among 16-to-29-year-olds and 92% among women. The NHS comes next at 79%, and 83% among the over-70s. Then the wider economy at 71%. Younger adults are also worried about housing (65%) and jobs (63%), and older adults about crime (69%) and global instability (64%). So rather than working only with Muslim groups, British Muslims can work with trade unions, tenants' associations, patient groups, local charities and interfaith networks: running food banks and warm hubs together, campaigning for NHS funding and affordable housing, and organising neighbourhood safety schemes with local residents.
