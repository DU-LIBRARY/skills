Part one · India

INDIA · PRINCIPLES 4–6

٤

Legal protections

An elected president, Muslim or not

Guaranteed rights, personal law, and protected mosques, awqāf and graveyards.

٥

Regional autonomy

A small centre: defence, foreign affairs, transport

Five Muslim-majority provinces make their own laws.

٦

Education

Religious education, even at extra tax

Shared schools, if the syllabus does not clash with Islam.

Fourth, constitutional and legal protections. He pictured a democratic constitution with an elected president, who could be Muslim or not, and a government that guaranteed political, economic and religious rights, personal law in the provinces where Muslims were a minority, and legal protection for mosques, awqāf, graveyards and Muslim culture. Fifth, local and regional autonomy. He wanted the centre limited to defence, foreign affairs, transport and some economic powers. Social and cultural matters would go to the provinces, and the five Muslim-majority provinces would be free to make their own laws on education, the economy and social and cultural life. Sixth, educational freedom and civic engagement. Education was a provincial matter, so Muslims in minority provinces could ask for special provision for religious education, even if it meant paying extra tax. Shared schools and colleges were fine as long as the syllabus didn't clash with Islamic values.
