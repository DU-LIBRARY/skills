Madanī’s principles

Part one · India

INDIA · PRINCIPLES 1–3

١

A uniting factor

Ending colonial rule

Muslims alone lacked the strength, so he united all Indians in one struggle.

٢

Equal citizenship

Equal rights and duties for Muslims and non-Muslims

And he said plainly: not an Islamic system of government.

٣

The lesser evil

Better than colonial rule, and better than partition

A joint government was never the end goal.

First, the uniting factor. He found one thing every Indian, Muslim or not, could unite around: ending colonial rule. He was a pragmatist. Muslims on their own didn't have the strength to remove the colonial power, so he worked to bring all Indians into the same struggle. Second, equal citizenship in a joint state. Whether independent India ended up united or federal, he said, it would be a joint government in which Muslims and non-Muslims had equal rights and equal duties. He was also clear that this would not be an Islamic system of government. Third, the lesser evil. For him, a united, independent India was better than colonial rule, because Muslims would have more room to uphold the truth and practise their religion. Islamic law couldn't be implemented under a joint government, but that government would still give Muslims the chance to show the superiority of Islamic law, in argument and in conduct, and to win non-Muslims over to it. He also preferred a united India to partition. Dividing the country, he warned, would let the colonial power exploit the rivalry and keep its grip on both, and the fear of non-Muslims in a united India was unfounded. And the joint government was never the end goal.
