INDIA · PRINCIPLES 7–9

٧

Moral effort

The lesser evil is not the goal

Iṣlāḥ centres and steady intellectual and moral work.

٨

Foreign policy

A foreign policy friendly to Islam

Indian Muslims better placed to help other Muslim lands.

٩

Conditional cooperation

Permissible and necessary, with safeguards

Governance, personal law, institutions and the five provinces.

Seventh, continuous moral and civic effort. A joint government wasn't going to implement the Sharīʿah, and the lesser evil wasn't the final goal. So Muslims still needed Iṣlāḥ (reform) centres and steady intellectual and moral work, with their eyes on the real goal. Eighth, principled foreign and public policy. Madanī argued that real Muslim participation at the centre, Muslim government in the provinces and ties with neighbouring Muslim countries would keep India's foreign policy friendly to Islam, and leave Indian Muslims better placed to help free other Muslim lands. Ninth and last, conditional cooperation. Working with non-Muslims for independence was permissible, and necessary, as long as certain things were safeguarded: Muslim participation in government, personal law, religious and cultural institutions, and self-rule in the five Muslim-majority provinces.
